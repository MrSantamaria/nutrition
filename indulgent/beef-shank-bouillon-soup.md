# Beef Shank Bouillon Soup

**Category:** Indulgent / off-plan meal
**Method:** Slow cooker (low 8–9 hrs, or high 5–6 hrs)
**Yield:** ~7 servings
**Effort:** Mostly dump and forget, one mid-cook step to skim fat

The bone-in shank gives the same fall-apart, melt-in-your-mouth texture as a
fattier cut like oxtail, but shank itself is naturally lean — most of the
richness in the broth comes from collagen breaking down, not fat. Skimming
the broth partway through keeps it from turning greasy.

---

## Ingredients

- 3–4 lbs bone-in beef shank (cross-cut), visible surface fat trimmed
- 8 cups water
- 4 Goya Chicken Bouillon Cubes
- 1 yellow onion, quartered
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 bay leaves
- 3 carrots, peeled and cut into large chunks
- 3 medium Yukon gold potatoes, peeled and quartered
- 8 oz cremini or button mushrooms, halved
- Salt and pepper, to taste
- Fresh cilantro, chopped (optional, for serving)

## Instructions

1. Trim as much visible surface fat off the shank as you reasonably can before cooking.
2. Into the slow cooker: shank, water, bouillon cubes, onion, garlic, bay leaves. Cook on **low 6–7 hrs** or **high 3–4 hrs**, until the meat is starting to pull away from the bone but isn't falling apart yet.
3. **Skim the fat.** Uncover and skim any fat pooled on the surface with a spoon (or ladle broth through a fat separator and return it). This is the step that keeps it from being greasy — do it now rather than at the end so the remaining cook time doesn't re-render more fat into the broth.
4. Add carrots and potatoes. Continue cooking on **low ~1.5–2 hrs** or **high ~45–60 min**, until the meat is fully fork-tender and the vegetables are cooked through.
5. Add mushrooms in the last 20–30 min so they don't turn to mush.
6. Remove bay leaves. Taste and season with salt and pepper — the bouillon cubes carry a lot of the salt already, so add gradually.
7. Serve hot, garnished with cilantro if using.

## Storage

Refrigerate up to 4–5 days, or freeze up to 3 months. If made ahead, chill uncovered until cold, then cover — any remaining fat solidifies into a cap on top that lifts off cleanly, making this even leaner as leftovers.

## Serving

Good on its own, or ladled over a scoop of rice. Crusty bread on the side if you want something to dip.

## Tips

- Shank is forgiving like chicken thighs — hard to overcook, since the connective tissue needs the long cook time to break down anyway.
- If you want it richer for the night your girlfriend's over, ladle the first-serving portions straight from the pot before it's fully chilled/skimmed (a little broth fat is part of what makes it taste indulgent); skim harder on the batch destined for the week's leftovers.
