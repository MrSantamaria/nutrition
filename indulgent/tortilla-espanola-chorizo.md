# Tortilla Española de Papas (Papa, Cebolla y Chorizo)

**Category:** Indulgent / off-plan meal — standalone brunch dish, not tied to a weekly plan
**Method:** Stovetop
**Yield:** 1 full tortilla, ~6 wedges
**Effort:** A bit of technique (the flip), but forgiving

Spanish potato omelet with onion and Spanish chorizo. **Use Spanish-style
chorizo** — the dry-cured, already-cooked sausage sold in a firm link,
sliced into rounds or half-moons. This is not Mexican chorizo, which is raw,
loose, and needs to be fully cooked and crumbled — the two aren't
interchangeable here.

---

## Ingredients

- 6 large eggs
- 2 lbs potatoes (Yukon gold or russet), peeled, thinly sliced
- 1 large yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 4–5 oz Spanish chorizo (cured), sliced into thin half-moons
- 1/2–3/4 cup olive oil (for slow-cooking the potatoes)
- Salt, to taste

## Instructions

1. Heat the olive oil in a large nonstick skillet over medium-low heat. Add the sliced potatoes and onion, season with a pinch of salt, and cook slowly — more of a gentle confit than a fry — stirring occasionally, until the potatoes are fork-tender but not browned or crispy. About 20–25 min.
2. Meanwhile, in a separate small pan, sauté the sliced chorizo over medium heat for 2–3 min, just until it releases some of its oil and lightly crisps at the edges. Set aside, keep the rendered chorizo oil.
3. Drain the potatoes and onion (save the olive oil — it's great for other cooking) and let cool slightly, a few minutes.
4. Whisk the eggs in a large bowl with a pinch of salt. Fold in the drained potatoes, onion, chorizo, and a spoonful of the reserved chorizo oil.
5. Wipe out the skillet, add 2 tbsp of the reserved oil, and heat over medium. Pour in the egg mixture, spreading evenly. Cook 3–4 min until the edges set and the bottom turns golden.
6. **The flip:** slide the tortilla onto a large plate. Invert the skillet on top of the plate, then flip both together so the tortilla drops back into the pan uncooked-side down. Go slow and use a plate a bit bigger than the pan — this is the only tricky step.
7. Cook another 3–4 min on the second side. Traditional tortilla española is left a little soft/custardy in the center — cook a bit longer if you prefer it fully set.
8. Slide onto a cutting board, rest 5 min, then slice into wedges.

## Storage

Refrigerate up to 3–4 days. Best served warm or at room temperature, not piping hot — this is normal for tortilla española, it's traditionally eaten that way as tapas.

## Serving

Stand-alone brunch dish with crusty bread and a simple salad, or cut into small cubes as tapas.

## Tips

- Slice potatoes thin and uniform (a mandoline helps) so they cook evenly at low heat.
- Don't skip draining the potatoes before mixing with egg — too much residual oil makes the tortilla greasy and harder to flip cleanly.
- If the flip feels risky, you can finish the top under a broiler for 1–2 min instead of flipping — less traditional, but foolproof.
